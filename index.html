Marcus Heppner's Favorite Movies

GoodFellas (1990). Directed By Martin Scorsese.

The true story of Henry Hill, a half-Irish, half-Sicillian Brooklyn kid who is adopted by neighborhood gangsters at an early age and climbs the ranks of a Mafia family under the guidance of Jimmy Conway. (Letterboxd)

Fun Fact: Edward McDonald, the actual U.S. attorney who placed the real Henry Hill into the witness protection program, played himself in the final interrogation scene.

Ratings

  • 93% on Rotten Tomatoes.
  • 8.7/10 on iMDB.
  • 4.5/5 on Letterboxd.

Why do I like GoodFellas

  • Quotable narration from Ray Liotta, as well as standout performances from Robert De Niro and Joe Pesci.
  • Gritty, unfiltered view into the life of a ganster, while stil remaining entertaining.
  • Memeroble soundtrack that leans on music from the correlated decades of the story, with my favorite being "Layla" by Derek and the Dominoes.

There Will Be Blood (2007). Directed By Paul Thomas Anderson.

Ruthless silver miner, turned oil prospecter, Daniel Plainview, moves to oil-rich California. Using his son to project a trustworthy, family-man image, Plainview cons local landowners into selling him their valuable properties for a pittance. However, local preacher Eli Sunday suspects Plainview's motives and intentions, starting a slow-burning fued that threatens both of their lives. (Letterboxd)

Fun Fact: The film was shot in Marfa, Texas, at the same time and general area as "No Country for Old Men," and a massive oil well smoke cloud from the set accidentally delayed filming for the Coen brothers' movie.

Ratings

  • 92% on Rotten Tomatoes.
  • 8.2/10 on iMDB.
  • 4.5/5 on Letterboxd.

Why do I like There Will Be Blood

  • Career-defining performance from Daniel Day-Lewis as Daniel Plainview.
  • Subverts the American Dream and "self-made man" narrative through Plainview's moral decay.

Apocalypse Now (1979). Directed By Francis Ford Coppola.

At the height of the Vietnam war, Captain Benjamin Willard is send on a dangerous mission that, officially, "does not exist, nor will it ever exist." His goal is to locate - and eliminate - a mysterious Green Beret Colonel named Walter Kurtz, who has been leading his personal army on illegal guerilla missions into enemy territory. (Letterboxd)

Fun Fact: Martin Sheen suffered a near-fatal heart attack during production. To keep the studio from shutting down the already ballooning project, the incident was covered up as "heat exhaustion."

Ratings

  • 91% on Rotten Tomatoes.
  • 8.4/10 on iMDB.
  • 4.4/5 on Letterboxd.

Why do I like Apocalypse Now

  • Symblolic storyline and descent down the Nung River parallels a descent into the darker depths of humanity.
  • Performances from Marlon Brando and Robert Duvall carry the film, despite less than 40 minutes of screen time between the two actors.
  • Historically troubled filming experience on location, which included monsoons in the Phillippines that destoryed sets.

The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly (1966). Directed By Sergio Leone.

While the Civil War rages on between the Union and the Confederacy, three men - a quient loner, a ruthless hitman, and a Mexican bandit - comb the American Southwest in search of a strongbox containing $200,000 in stolen gold.

Fun Fact: Clint Eastwood kept the iconic poncho/serape he wore throughout the Dollars Trilogy. He has never washed it to this day.

Ratings

  • 99% on Rotten Tomatoes.
  • 8.8/10 on iMDB.
  • 4.5/5 on Letterboxd.

Why do I like The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly

  • Legendary soundtrack from Ennio Morricone, including the iconic main theme and "Ecstasy of Gold."
  • Large-scale story following three main characters, with one of the most epic standoffs and conclusions in film history.
  • Analysis of the many levels of evil, most notably greed, in the context of the Civil War.
